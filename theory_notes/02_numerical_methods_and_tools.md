# Numerical Methods, Algorithms & Software Stack in Relativistic Astrophysics

This document outlines the numerical algorithms, discretization strategies, and computational ecosystem employed by the IAC3/UIB group to solve the coupled Einstein–GRMHD–Radiation equations at scale.

## 2. Spatial Discretization Strategies (Method of Lines)

Because spacetime metric fields are smooth while fluid/radiation fields develop sharp shocks and contact discontinuities, the group uses a **hybrid spatial discretization** within the Method of Lines (MoL)[cite: 241, 280, 290]:

### 2.1. Spacetime & SGS Derivatives (Smooth Sector)
* **4th-order centered finite differences** for spatial derivatives in CCZ4 and SGS tensors[cite: 280, 290]:
  $$\partial_x U_i = \frac{1}{12\Delta x}(U_{i-2} - 8U_{i-1} + 8U_{i+1} - U_{i+2}) + \mathcal{O}(\Delta x^4)$$
* **Upwinded 4th-order finite differences** for shift advection terms $\beta^k \partial_k U$ (direction chosen by the sign of $\beta^k$)[cite: 290].
* **6th-order Kreiss–Oliger (KO) artificial dissipation** to filter unresolved high-frequency grid modes without spoiling 4th-order convergence[cite: 280, 291]:
  $$Q_d^x U_i = \frac{\sigma}{64\Delta x}(U_{i-3} - 6U_{i-2} + 15U_{i-1} - 20U_i + 15U_{i+1} - 6U_{i+2} + U_{i+3})$$

### 2.2. Fluid & Radiation Fluxes (Shock-Capturing Sector — HRSC)
Conservation laws $\partial_t U + \partial_k F^k(U) = S(U)$ are discretized in conservative finite-difference/finite-volume form[cite: 291]:
$$\partial_x F \approx \frac{1}{\Delta x}\left(\hat{F}_{i+1/2} - \hat{F}_{i-1/2}\right)$$
1. **Flux Splitting (Local Lax–Friedrichs / Rusanov or HLLE):**
   $$F_i^\pm = \frac{1}{2}(F_i \pm \lambda U_i)$$
   where $\lambda$ is the local maximum characteristic wave speed[cite: 31, 291].
2. **High-Order Reconstruction:**
   * **MP5 (5th-order Monotonicity-Preserving):** Default choice in `MHDuet` for BNS/BHNS mergers[cite: 280, 292].
   * **WENO3YC / WENO5Z:** Weighted Essentially Non-Oscillatory schemes used in turbulence and generalized induction benchmarks[cite: 197, 219].
   * **PPM (Piecewise Parabolic Method):** Used in `WhiskyRMHD`[cite: 31].

## 3. Time Integration & Stiffness Handling

### 3.1. Explicit Runge–Kutta (Non-Stiff Sector)
* **4th-order Runge–Kutta (RK4)** with Courant factor $\Delta t \le 0.4\,\Delta x$[cite: 280, 290].
* **Berger–Oliger Sub-cycling in Time:** Finer AMR levels advance with smaller $\Delta t$ than coarser levels, using high-order space-time interpolation at refinement boundaries[cite: 241, 280].

### 3.2. IMEX Runge–Kutta (Stiff Relaxation Terms)
In **resistive GRMHD** ($\sigma \gg 1$) and **M1 neutrino transport** (optically thick core, $\kappa_\nu \Delta t \gg 1$), explicit time-stepping becomes unstable unless $\Delta t \propto 1/\sigma$ or $1/\kappa_\nu$[cite: 31, 32].
* **Solution — IMEX-RK (e.g., IMEX-SSP3(4,3,3) L-stable):**
  Splits the PDE into non-stiff hyperbolic fluxes $F(U)$ (integrated explicitly) and stiff local relaxation sources $\frac{1}{\epsilon}R(U)$ (integrated implicitly)[cite: 32, 33]:
  $$U^{(i)} = U^n + \Delta t \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \tilde{a}_{ij} F(U^{(j)}) + \Delta t \sum_{j=1}^{N} a_{ij} \frac{1}{\epsilon} R(U^{(j)})$$
* Because $R(U)$ is purely local to each cell, the implicit step reduces to a $3 \times 3$ matrix inversion per cell (for $E^i$ in resistive MHD[cite: 33] or $F^i$ in M1 radiation), keeping $\Delta t$ governed solely by the speed of light CFL condition.
* **Asymptotic Preserving (AP) Fluxes (for M1):** In optically thick cells ($\kappa \Delta x \gg 1$), characteristic wave speeds inside the Riemann solver are rescaled so numerical dissipation $\propto c\Delta x$ does not swamp physical neutrino diffusion $D \propto 1/(3\kappa)$.

## 4. Constraint Enforcement & Variable Recovery

### 4.1. Maintaining $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$
Instead of staggered grids (Constrained Transport), `MHDuet` and `WhiskyRMHD` evolve all field components at cell centers and use **Hyperbolic Divergence Cleaning**[cite: 30, 195, 255]:
* Couples a scalar damping potential $\phi$ to the induction equation so divergence errors propagate away at speed $c_h$ and decay exponentially on a timescale $1/\kappa$[cite: 30, 195].

### 4.2. Conservative-to-Primitive Inversion (`Cons2Prim`)
At every RK substep, primitive variables $\{\rho, \epsilon, T, Y_e, v^i\}$ must be recovered from conserved variables $\{D, D_Y, \tau, S_i, B^i\}$ by solving a system of coupled non-linear transcendental equations[cite: 281]:
1. **Enforce Physical Bounds:** Check $D \ge D_{\mathrm{vac}}$ (tenuous artificial atmosphere floor, $\sim 10^4 - 10^7\,\mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}$) and $S^2 \le (D+\tau)^2$[cite: 241, 255, 281, 296].
2. **Primary Fast 3D Newton–Raphson Solver:** Solves simultaneously for $\{W, z \equiv hW^2, T\}$ using the tabulated EOS $p(\rho, T, Y_e)$ and $\epsilon(\rho, T, Y_e)$[cite: 281].
3. **Fallback Robust 1D Bracketed Solver (Kastaun et al. / Brent's method):** If 3D Newton–Raphson fails in low-density, highly magnetized regions, a 1D root-finder guaranteed to converge inside analytical brackets is triggered[cite: 241, 281, 282].
4. **Black Hole Interior Treatment:** Inside the apparent horizon ($\alpha < \alpha_0 \approx 0.15$), smooth exponential damping $G(\alpha) = \exp[-(\alpha/\alpha_0)^p]$ is applied to fluid variables to prevent `Cons2Prim` failures near punctures[cite: 315].

### 4.3. Eikonal Solver for Optical Depth
To compute non-local neutrino optical depths $\tau_\nu$ across arbitrary 3D AMR grids without ray-tracing bottlenecks, `MHDuet` solves the curved-space Eikonal equation $|\nabla \tau_\nu| = \kappa_\nu$ iteratively via a fast sweeping / quadratic upwind finite-difference scheme[cite: 282, 283].

## 5. Verification & Validation (V&V) Workflow

Every new module or physics extension in the group follows a strict validation hierarchy before production BNS/BHNS runs:
1. **1D Benchmarks:** Circularly polarized Alfvén waves (smooth convergence order), self-similar current sheets (resistive diffusion), Brio–Wu / relativistic shock tubes (shock capturing across $\sigma \in [0, 10^6]$)[cite: 34, 35, 36].
2. **2D/3D Box Turbulence & Blast Waves:** Cylindrical/spherical blast waves, 3D Kelvin–Helmholtz instability (verifying Kolmogorov $k^{-5/3}$ kinetic and Kazantsev $k^{3/2}$ magnetic energy spectra, plus *a-priori* and *a-posteriori* SGS calibration)[cite: 37, 222, 223, 228].
3. **Isolated Relativistic Stars (TOV):** Static and rapidly rotating magnetized NSs in Cowling and dynamical spacetimes (comparing $f$-mode and overtone frequencies against linear perturbation theory, and testing neutrino cooling luminosities)[cite: 39, 40, 283, 284].
4. **Collapse to a Black Hole:** Unstable TOV star collapsing to a Kerr/Schwarzschild BH, checking horizon formation, electromagnetic Poynting luminosity, and ringdown Quasi-Normal Mode (QNM) frequencies[cite: 42, 43, 45].
5. **Self-Convergence Testing:** Running at 3 resolutions ($\Delta x_0, \Delta x_1, \Delta x_2$) and measuring the convergence order $n_c = \log_r \left(\frac{\|U_0 - U_1\|_1}{\|U_1 - U_2\|_1}\right)$ on $L_1$-norms and gravitational waveform phases[cite: 47, 197, 291].
